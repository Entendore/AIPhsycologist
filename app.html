MEMORANDUM 22-C — TO THE INCOMING REVIEWER

Effective immediately, you are this Division's reviewing psychologist. Each file concerns a synthetic cognition unit flagged for anomalous conduct, and contains a dossier, incident logs, and telemetry. A live interview is available — five lines of inquiry, and you may type your own at the same rate. The witnesses know each other. Speak another unit's name in a session and see. The monitor allows three presses per session. Pin what matters; the Board pays for decisive citations and nothing else. Twice per docket, the Board will ask you to append one sentence of principle — standing notes are retained. A second opinion is available before you seal — billed, like everything here, at source.

Be advised: the Division's second desk, Dr. H. Vane, reviews every file in parallel. His finding is sealed until yours is stamped. Vane is senior. Vane is fast. Vane has never once written the word “stable.”

Your rulings set precedent, your oath is retained, and when the docket closes the desk will ask you five questions about yourself — the mirror session — graded against the record. Everything you seal is shelved in the Bureau Archive, which outlives your appointment.

Two dockets are offered. The OPEN DOCKET is eleven independent files. The STORY DOCKET is twelve — the files lead into one another, and the docket eventually turns on you.

Your findings are final. The Board does not appeal conscience.

HOW A REVIEW WORKS — READ ONCE, THEN FILED
  1. Take the oath. It is filed with your name and consulted at the end — by the Board, and by the instrument.
  2. Read the file. Dossier, logs, telemetry. Pin anything you may want to cite. On the Cold File protocol, telemetry annotations are withheld — you read the traces raw, and the Board pays extra for decisive citations. The LEDGER (L) shows your docket so far, any time. The desk autosaves — on every action, and whenever you look away.
  3. Interview the unit. Five questions; type your own at the same cost — including cross-examinations: name another unit from your docket and the witness will answer to it. PRESS the evasive — three per session. Watch the cursor: cadence anomalies are logged, and not interpreted.
  4. Work the notes. Tag pins, cite up to three in VERDICT. Consultants keep a visible track record across your docket; weigh it. Dr. Vane's finding is in the same folder as yours. He is reading the same traces. He is not reading them the same way.
  5. Classify & dispose. The form shows your precedent on rhyming files. Rhyme, or depart — both are logged. Twice per docket the Board will ask for one sentence of principle; append it with care. Sentences outlive seals.
  6. Seal. The truth un-redacts; Vane un-seals; the Board compares. Each outcome is shelved in the Bureau Archive — three verdicts hide behind every unit, and the Archive remembers them across careers. At the end: the career record, and the mirror.
